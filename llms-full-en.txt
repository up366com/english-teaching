## Services

### Speaking Practice & Assessment

Students complete reading aloud, situational Q&A and topic presentation online. The engine locates problems at the level of word stress, linking and intonation, provides model pronunciation and targeted practice, and turns progress into a report teachers can teach from.

Speaking is the easiest skill to hide behind a feeling: students think they read fluently, and a teacher cannot listen to a whole class inside 45 minutes. Tianxuewang breaks the problem into three steps: assess, diagnose, recommend.
Assess. Students complete reading aloud, situational Q&A or topic presentation tasks aligned to the curriculum standard. The engine analyses each sentence using natural language processing and multi-dimensional speech analysis. It does not merely flag a mistake — it locates it: which syllable carries the stress, where a linking should have occurred, where a falling tone was needed but a rise was produced.
Diagnose. Results are aggregated into a capability radar chart and an individual report. Students see "my weakness is linking and intonation" instead of an isolated number. Teachers see the distribution of class-level problems — which sound category, which task type, which class needs the most feedback.
Recommend. Targeted practice is pushed automatically: unstable stress gets stress drills, broken linking gets linking drills, flat intonation gets model-sentence shadowing. Practice is followed by another assessment, forming an assess–diagnose–recommend–re-assess loop rather than a one-off test.
The loop covers classroom practice, after-class training and lab-based mock exams with one scoring standard, so classroom data and exam results are directly comparable. For teachers, the point is not another scoring tool — it is evidence they can teach from.

### AI Writing Assessment

Powered by the in-house model, it annotates grammar, vocabulary and spelling issues while evaluating logic and structure, aggregates class-level patterns, and gives each student actionable revision advice.

Marking essays is the heaviest repetitive work in English teaching. Fifty books, two or three hours, and when it is done the feedback is hard to deliver — the problems are scattered, so a teacher reads out a few examples and moves on.
Tianxuewang separates marking from teaching. Marking goes to the model: grammar, collocation and spelling are annotated sentence by sentence, and the essay is also evaluated for logical progression and paragraph structure — whether claims are supported, whether paragraphs connect. Teaching stays with the teacher: the system aggregates the class into a single view, so the dominant error type and the weakest stage are immediately visible.
The efficiency difference is an order of magnitude. A class that used to take hours is marked in five to ten minutes. The time saved goes into designing feedback and one-to-one support — which is where workload reduction actually becomes teaching improvement.
For students, marking is not just a score. Every annotation maps to a revision suggestion, so the next draft has a clear target. Teachers can project a typical problem and revise one paragraph live, which works better than reading out a model essay.
Results connect to the teaching platform: errors and weaknesses enter the student's practice record and inform the next homework set and targeted drills, so marking does not end up archived and unused.

### English Teaching Platform

A single workspace for teachers: vocabulary lists, grammar micro-lessons, reading materials and synced practice, with learning data updating as the class progresses to support differentiated teaching.

The problem a teaching platform should solve is not a shortage of resources but their dispersion. Lesson preparation usually means switching between textbook, slides, question bank and audio files, and the preparation time is fragmented in the process.
Tianxuewang's teaching platform gathers the everyday materials into one workspace: vocabulary lists synced to the textbook, grammar micro-lessons, reading passages and matching practice, organised by unit and difficulty. Teachers can use them as they are, or adjust difficulty and volume for a specific class.
Homework runs through assignment, submission, marking and feedback in one flow. Students work on phone or tablet; teachers see completion rate, accuracy and typical errors, and marking results feed back into learning data without manual tallying.
The analytics dashboard is where the platform earns its place. At class level it shows shared weaknesses; at individual level it shows the trajectory of each capability. A teacher can split a class into two or three ability groups and assign different tasks — differentiated teaching becomes an option in the homework dialog rather than a slogan.
For the teaching group, the platform is also a container: school-made study guides, topic drills and feedback slides stay on the platform, so a new teacher does not start from zero.

### Listening, Vocabulary & Dictation

From graded listening and full-screen walkthroughs to dictation practice and error-paper rebuilding, plus dictation marking and a class error-word book — one chain covering comprehension, spelling and retention.

Listening and vocabulary are the two areas where results are hardest to see: students listen and memorise, but where they went wrong and whether it stuck is rarely clear. This product line connects the three stages.
Comprehension. Graded listening is synced to the coursebook, and teachers can adjust playback speed to match the class — useful as exam listening speeds up. The full-screen walkthrough helps locate high-frequency errors, search for similar items in one click, and play short audio segments repeatedly, so explaining a question no longer means dragging a progress bar.
Dictation. Teachers edit the transcript online and blank any word to build a dictation task, then export and print it. Students answer on a phone; the system marks instantly and produces a complete report showing accuracy and the distribution of misspelled words.
Vocabulary. Word lists are synced to the textbook, with definitions, example sentences, roots and affixes, and pronunciation-pattern training. Dictation uses the Tianxuewang dictation sheet: the teacher scans or photographs by class and marks a full class in two to three minutes, with an automatic report summarising high-frequency errors by grade and class.
The final step is re-exposure: high-frequency errors enter the class error-word book, where the paper-building tool puts them back into contextual item types for a second round, and error papers can be rebuilt for assignment or printing. Listening, spelling and retention close the loop — which is the only way to compete with the forgetting curve.

### Three common misunderstandings about AI essay marking

Published: 2026-02-10

AI marking has been part of English writing instruction for several years, yet discussion still centres on three questions.
Misunderstanding one: machine scores are unreliable, so it is useless. If the score is all you look at, the criticism holds. But the value lies in diagnosis, not the number. The system flags "paragraph three lacks a transition" or "this collocation is wrong" — information a teacher can verify, teach from, or reject. Treating AI marking as a scoring machine uses its weakest capability.
Misunderstanding two: too much annotation, so students ignore it. That is true — give thirty annotations at once and a student reads only the total score. The effective approach is layered presentation: three key revision suggestions on the first screen, with the rest available in detail. That is exactly what Teacher Edition 6.0 does: nothing removed, order changed.
Misunderstanding three: teachers will be replaced. Marking replaces the repetitive work of paging through books and flagging errors; it does not replace teaching. How a lesson is run, which paragraph to discuss, what students revise afterwards — these still depend on a teacher's judgement about the class. Marking instead frees teachers from clerical labour so they have time to design feedback.
Behind all three is the same assumption: treating AI as an automated teacher. A more accurate view is a verifiable teaching assistant — it supplies evidence, the teacher judges; it handles repetition, the teacher designs.

### Practising speaking under the new curriculum: turning feeling into evidence

Published: 2026-01-15

China's new English curriculum standard sets more specific expectations for speaking and listening: not only saying something, but saying it clearly and appropriately. In the classroom the problem is practical — with dozens of students, who has which problem, and how do you see it within one lesson?
We suggest breaking speaking practice into three operational moves.
First, fix the task types. Reading aloud, situational Q&A and topic presentation test different abilities: pronunciation and intonation, information retrieval and response, organisation and coherence. Choose one or two per session rather than a little of everything, which measures nothing well.
Second, fix the scoring standard. The same standard must run through classroom practice, homework and mock exams, or the data cannot be compared. If the classroom uses one standard and the exam another, progress cannot be verified and practice degenerates into reading it again.
Third, fix the re-test rhythm. Practice must be followed by another assessment, or the diagnosis never closes. Tianxuewang's loop is assess–diagnose–recommend–re-assess: identify the problem, generate a radar chart and report, assign targeted practice, then re-test the same task type and look at the change.
None of these requires expensive equipment; they require instructional design. The role of technology is to turn feeling into evidence: a teacher no longer says "this class has weak intonation" from impression but sees the class distribution on the intonation dimension; a student no longer says "I read fine" but knows which linking to work on.

### After marking a class in five to ten minutes, where does the teacher's time go?

Published: 2025-09-10

Every Teachers' Day, the discussion about teacher workload returns to the same numbers: how many exercise books, essays and dictation sheets an English teacher marks each week. Since AI marking arrived, those numbers have genuinely fallen — a class set of essays from hours to five to ten minutes.
But we observe two very different outcomes in schools.
In the first, the time saved is reinvested in teaching: the teacher uses the marking report to locate class-level patterns, designs a fifteen-minute feedback session, projects a typical paragraph and revises it live, then spends the rest of the time with three or five students individually. Marking results become input for teaching decisions.
In the second, the time saved is filled with new repetitive work: one more practice set, one more round of papers, one more form. The efficiency tool changed speed but not structure, and the sense of workload did not fall.
The difference is not the tool but the way it is used. In teacher training we emphasise one move: read the aggregated view before deciding what to teach. Skip that step and marking becomes faster scoring, while the diagnostic information in the report is wasted.
This is especially true in writing. What students need is not a score but a target for the next draft. One actionable revision suggestion is worth ten remarks about "unnatural language". That is why we restructured the marking report around revision advice rather than point deductions.

### 4.88 billion speaking assessments: the engineering behind the scale

Published: 2025-04-22

Tianxuewang's speaking assessment engine has passed 4.88 billion cumulative calls. The figure is usually read as a scale metric; for a product team it is closer to a stress-test report.
Beyond the billion-call mark, the hard problems are no longer "can it score" but three others.
Consistency. The same passage from the same student, submitted on a different device, network or day, should receive a stable score. Microphones, ambient noise and recording gain all affect acoustic features; if those factors enter the score, assessment loses its diagnostic value.
Granularity. "Pronunciation is not standard" is useless feedback. The engine must locate the problem: which syllable carries the stress, where a linking was expected but broken, where a final fall was replaced by a rise — and provide a model to imitate. Granularity determines whether a teacher can act on it.
Explainability. A student sees a score; a teacher should see a capability radar chart and a class distribution. Results must be explainable, discussable and convertible into practice, not a closed-box output.
4.88 billion calls also represent a data asset: the distribution of pronunciation problems across regions, year groups and task types. That distribution in turn supports the targeted practice library — once the system knows a linking error is frequent in a given stage, recommendation stops being random matching.

For text, it must go beyond grammar and vocabulary errors to judge whether an argument holds and whether paragraphs connect. For recommendation, it must combine knowledge-graph relations with learning behaviour data to decide what item comes next.
Released alongside the model was Teacher Edition 6.0. The point of the upgrade was not more features but fewer jumps: planning, assignment, marking and feedback were consolidated into one workflow, so teachers no longer switch between modules. Marking results surface directly as "class-level patterns" and "typical paragraphs", and one click opens the feedback view.
The product team mentioned a detail at launch: early marking reports were comprehensive, and teachers did not finish reading them. Version 6.0 changed the first screen to three things — the most concentrated errors this time, the paragraph worth teaching, and targeted practice that can be assigned immediately. Nothing was removed; the order changed.
The model and Teacher Edition 6.0 became the base for later products: writing marking, speaking practice, listening and vocabulary, and the teaching platform share one set of model capabilities and one teacher workflow — the concrete form of "model plus agent" among the company's three pillars.

### From listening books to a gold award: the print foundation beneath the platform

Published: 2023-06-18

In 2023 Tianxue English books received a gold award for high-efficiency study aids. Earlier, the smart edition of Bolan English Listening won a gold award in the topic-specific category of the 2018–2019 study-aid brand rankings.
This history is often overlooked, yet it explains a defining feature of the products: the content did not emerge from an algorithm, it accumulated over years of educational publishing. How to select listening material, how to grade speed, how to design items, where to place blanks — those judgements were refined in the print era.
Digitalisation gave that content new uses. In listening, for example: book content syncs with graded listening, and teachers can adjust playback speed for a class; the full-screen walkthrough cuts audio into short segments tied to high-frequency errors; the dictation builder lets teachers edit the transcript, blank any word and export for printing. The same content serves different stages in print and digital form.
This also means the product logic is not "replace paper with technology" but "make paper content computable". Students answer on a dictation sheet, the teacher photographs it, and the system marks a class in two to three minutes — paper is not abandoned; marking and tallying are automated.
For schools, that continuity matters: the material system a teaching group already knows is not disrupted, and teachers do not have to replace all their content in order to use new technology.
